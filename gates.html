How to play

Each day you get one circuit. Inputs A and B run through two gates whose outputs are C and D, which feed a final gate that produces Q. Work out what Q is for all four input combinations and fill in the Q column — tap a cell to cycle empty → 0 → 1.

Gates are drawn with their standard schematic symbols — each one is shown beside its truth table below. A small circle (○) is a NOT bubble: on an input wire it flips that input before it reaches the gate, and on a gate's tip it inverts the output (that tip bubble is what turns AND, OR, and XOR into NAND, NOR, and XNOR).

The C and D columns are optional scratch paper for working through the two middle wires. They are never checked and never affect your result.

You have three tries. A submission only tells you correct or not — no hints about which rows are wrong. Only wrong answers cost a life: the winning submission is free, and duplicate or incomplete submissions are rejected without spending anything.

When your day is done, Share result copies a spoiler-free summary to your clipboard — the day number and one ✅ or ❌ per try — ready to paste anywhere.

The six gates

In the equations below, AB means A AND B (multiplication), A + B means A OR B, a bar over an expression means NOT (flip it), and ⊕ is exclusive-OR.

AND — Q = AB

Outputs 1 only when both inputs are 1 — like two switches in series: both must be on for current to flow. A single 0 anywhere forces the output to 0.
